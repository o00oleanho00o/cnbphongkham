## Public host and access observations

- `https://annam.vttechsolution.vn/` returned a login redirect shell; no public feature data was visible. This is an access boundary, not evidence of a product defect.
- `https://vttechsolution.vn/` and `https://vttech.vn/` returned 404 during this check. The currently reachable marketing domain was `https://vttechsolution.com/`.
- The documentation page loaded but showed a JavaScript “Loading...” shell and no accessible case-study details in the HTML response.
- No public customer complaint or independent review specific to VTTech was verified in this bounded pass. Do not turn the absence of indexed complaints into a positive quality claim.

## Evidence-based gap hypotheses for Pema validation

These are hypotheses generated by comparing public positioning with Pema's requested workflow; they are **not verified complaints**:

1. **Breadth versus longitudinal clinical context.** A system can cover appointments, records, prescriptions, services, inventory and revenue yet still make a doctor reconstruct a multi-session aesthetic journey manually. Test whether Patient 360 can show concern → treatment session → standardized photos → response → aftercare → next action in under 30 seconds.
2. **Patient app versus treatment companion.** The public Customer App evidence includes booking, treatment history, service information, images, loyalty and automatic notifications. Test depth: can patients see a treatment plan, aftercare and medication/skincare instructions, submit a follow-up photo and receive a reviewed response without Zalo/paper workarounds?
3. **Integration breadth versus workflow ownership.** SMS/Zalo/call-center/e-invoice/accounting/imaging integrations are valuable, but integration presence alone does not prove that a receptionist can resolve “arrived/waiting/missing consent/photo/payment” in one queue. Observe handoffs and duplicate entry at Pema.
4. **Marketing feature labels versus specialty protocols.** Public pages mention “bệnh án - điều trị” and “dịch vụ - sản phẩm”, but do not publicly show dermatology-specific photo protocols (same body area/angle/lighting), treatment milestones, adverse reaction triage or missed-follow-up detection. Validate these explicitly before deciding whether VTTech can be configured or must be complemented.
5. **Documentation discoverability.** The public documentation endpoint exposes categories but returned no usable article content in this fetch. Confirm with a real trial/demo whether staff can self-serve or need vendor support for common workflow changes.

## What to copy, integrate or test instead of rebuilding

- **Copy the category framing:** one clinic operating system plus a customer app and Zalo surface, because Vietnamese clinics already expect local communication channels.
- **Integrate where possible:** e-invoice, payment/accounting, SMS/Zalo delivery and national e-prescription interfaces should not be re-created in a Pema prototype. Use adapters and an audit trail.
- **Differentiate in the clinic workflow:** Patient 360, standardized clinical photography, treatment milestones, follow-up inbox and doctor-reviewed practical AI. These are not established by the public VTTech pages and need direct workflow testing.

## Questions for a VTTech demo or Pema workshop

1. Can a doctor see all treatment sessions, photos, aftercare and patient-reported reactions in one chronological view?
2. Does a patient-submitted photo create a review task with SLA/owner, and can the doctor approve a response?
3. How are standardized image sets linked to body area, treatment stage and consent?
4. What happens when a patient misses a planned follow-up or a treatment session is completed without aftercare sent?
5. Can staff change treatment templates, consent forms, statuses and notification rules without vendor engineering?
6. Which integrations are included in the quoted plan, and which require separate modules or manual export?
