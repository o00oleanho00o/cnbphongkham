## What the official Pema site says

Pema positions itself as a specialist dermatology and aesthetics clinic, with the promise “Điều trị - Chăm sóc da đơn giản, khoa học và hiệu quả đến từ sự thấu hiểu làn da.” Its about page names three P values — Professional, Perfect — alongside understanding, expertise, science, care and sustainability. The visible patient promise is clinical trust and long-term accompaniment, not a generic beauty marketplace.

The homepage has a strong blue identity: primary `#0B4F94`, supporting light blue `#3CAAE5`, white and very pale cool grey. Headings use the brand's FS Magistral face; body/navigation use Be Vietnam Pro. The official logo is the blue `Pema+ clinic & spa` mark from the site's own image asset. Pema's public navigation prioritizes doctors, dermatology conditions, high-tech aesthetics, community projects, customer stories, knowledge and news.

## Product implication

The internal tool should be Pema-branded but quieter than the marketing site. Use the blue identity for brand, focus and primary actions; reserve saturated status colors for meaning. Use Vietnamese headings with Be Vietnam Pro or a close local sans. A small Pema logo/wordmark is appropriate; a fictional green “Pema” brand is not.

## What Fastboy/Go Check In is actually good at

The official product page describes a closed loop: phone-number check-in → recognized customer → captured contact and visit history → automated SMS reminders/campaigns → review request → loyalty/return visit. Go Booking adds 24/7 booking, reminders, deposits, staff calendars and no-show reduction. The important idea is ownership of the customer lifecycle and measurable next action, rather than the POS table itself.

## What maps to Pema

1. Check-in by phone number should safely find a patient profile, but identity/consent and duplicate matching must be explicit.
2. Every completed visit should emit a next action: aftercare due, D1/D3/D7 check, photo requested, doctor review or next appointment.
3. Patient segmentation can be clinical workflow segments (new, active, follow-up due, overdue, reaction reported), not marketing labels only.
4. Booking/reminder and deposit status can be an adapter around the clinic's existing schedule and payment flow.
5. Retention analytics should measure care quality: follow-up completed, response time, no-show/rebook, photo protocol completion and next visit.
6. A review request can be offered only after a safe care interaction and with consent; it must never replace symptom triage or pressure a patient.

## What must not be copied blindly

- Automated “happy customer” review targeting cannot decide that a clinical case is safe; doctor/staff escalation wins.
- Birthday/promotion SMS is secondary to aftercare, reaction triage and consent.
- “30M members” network and salon loyalty points do not create a dermatology moat.
- A single check-in event is not the same as a clinical visit: Pema needs episode, treatment session, image metadata, aftercare, patient report and owner.
- Public claims about retention or revenue should not be carried into Pema without baseline measurement and consent/legal review.

## Working thesis

The strongest Pema wedge is **Care Loop OS**: phone/Zalo check-in → Patient 360 → treatment/session → approved aftercare → D1/D3/D7 task → patient update/photo → triage/review → next appointment. It extends the existing Patient 360 + Follow-up Inbox direction into a measurable lifecycle without turning the first release into a marketing CRM.
